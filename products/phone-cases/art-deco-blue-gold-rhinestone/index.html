The Design

Every arc on this case repeats the same fan-shaped curve — layered, mirrored and stacked until it reads as a single continuous pattern. That repetition is deliberate: it's the defining visual language of Art Deco, the early-20th-century style built on machine-age geometry, symmetry and controlled ornament.

The color pairing carries its own weight. A deep slate-blue base sits under sharp gold lines — a combination that reads as composed and a little formal, the kind of contrast associated with classic, high-end design rather than anything trend-driven. Along every curve and edge, fine rhinestones are set into the gold linework, the same "gold-and-gem" logic used in fine jewelry, pushed here into an everyday object.

Underneath the surface, fine glitter and star-shaped sequins sit in the clear resin layer — not part of the printed pattern, but a separate depth that catches light as the case turns in hand. It softens the geometry's precision with something closer to movement, so the case reads differently under different light rather than staying static.
