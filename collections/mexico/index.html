Mexico Culture

This collection spans two product lines carrying eighteen Mexican pattern stories. Four motifs live on a matte, biodegradable wheat-straw shell as a same-colour 3D relief; fourteen more — spanning Día de los Muertos icons, craft traditions, wrestling culture and Mesoamerican mythology — live on a soft-touch, fleece-lined liquid silicone shell as a same-colour laser-engraved, flush-smooth finish. Neither is a print: both hold their pattern as texture, not ink. Built for retailers and gift buyers serving Mexico and the wider Latin American market who want cultural depth across more than one material story.

Material Biodegradable · Laser-Engraved

#DYF2452 · Biodegradable · Laser-Engraved Aztec Geometry Biodegradable Case

Four building blocks of Aztec and Maya design — the stepped pyramid as a sacred mountain, the sun stone as the turning of time, the stepped-fret spiral as movement, and the diamond lattice as an ordered cosmos. Together: cosmic order and a civilisation that has lasted.
#DYF2453 · Biodegradable · Laser-Engraved Alebrijes Art Biodegradable Case

The alebrije is a Mexican folk-art invention: a fantastical creature built from many animals at once — feline body, wings, horns, a serpent tail — covered in dense scrollwork. Born from a craftsman's fever dream in 1930s Mexico City, it's read as a spirit guide between worlds.
#DYF2454 · Biodegradable · Laser-Engraved Floral Embroidery Biodegradable Case

The motif comes from Mexican hand embroidery — Oaxacan scrolling flowers and Otomí Tenango bird work — with a hummingbird in flight at its centre. Flowers stand for abundance and pride; the colibrí, for joy and love; the stitching itself, for patience handed down.
#DYF2455 · Biodegradable · Laser-Engraved Mayan Glyphs Biodegradable Case

The pattern sets Maya hieroglyph cartouches against Kukulkan, the feathered serpent that descends El Castillo's staircase at the equinox. The glyphs stand for knowledge carved to outlast its makers; the serpent, for renewal and cycles.

Material Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved

#DYF2456 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Catrina Calavera Silicone Case

La Catrina's sugar-skull portrait, framed in a marigold crown and lace-like linework — Mexico's best-known Día de los Muertos icon, honouring death with colour and ceremony rather than fear.
#DYF2457 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Papel Picado Silicone Case

Papel picado's perforated banner motif, echoing Mexico's festival and wedding decorations — a cut-paper craft whose own logic of removing material to reveal a pattern maps naturally onto laser engraving.
#DYF2458 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Talavera Tile Silicone Case

A Talavera-style floral medallion and geometric border, referencing Puebla's UNESCO-listed glazed-ceramic craft — one of the most recognisable handmade-Mexico visual signatures.
#DYF2459 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Lucha Libre Mask Silicone Case

A front-facing lucha libre mask design — the identity a Mexican wrestler builds a career around — rendered as bold engraved linework for a younger, street-culture audience.
#DYF2460 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Sarape Stripe Silicone Case

A Saltillo sarape blanket's central diamond medallion and zigzag stripe bands, translated from a woven textile into pure engraved linework.
#DYF2461 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Cempasúchil Marigold Silicone Case

Cempasúchil, the marigold that guides spirits home during Día de los Muertos — rendered as layered petal linework, a specific and story-carrying alternative to a generic floral print.
#DYF2462 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Milagros Folk Charms Silicone Case

Milagros — the small votive charms Mexican Catholics leave at church altars for protection and blessing — arranged as an engraved pattern that carries an emotional charge beyond decoration.
#DYF2463 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Quetzalcóatl Silicone Case

Quetzalcóatl, the feathered serpent worshipped across Mesoamerican civilisations as a god of wisdom, wind and life — a specific mythological figure rather than a generic geometric pattern.
#DYF2464 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Barro Negro Silicone Case

Barro negro, Oaxaca's hand-carved black-clay pottery craft — a rustic bird-and-vine motif that gives the Mexico collection a second, more handmade texture alongside the refined Talavera tile design.

The eagle perched on a nopal cactus, serpent in its beak — the founding legend at the centre of Mexico's flag, and the most direct, least-explaining-required Mexican symbol there is.
#DYF2466 · Liquid Silicone · Laser-Engraved Aztec Geometry Silicone Case
